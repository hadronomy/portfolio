# Spanish CV profile research

This note records the source-backed language decisions for the Spanish
`Perfil profesional`. It separates guidance from editorial choices. The
profile must describe the same work as the English source data and must fit a
one-page technical CV.

## Source guidance

- [Instituto Cervantes: «Adecuación»](https://cvc.cervantes.es/ensenanza/biblioteca_ele/diccio_ele/diccionario/adecuacion.htm)
  defines adequacy as adapting the language to the subject, channel,
  relationship with the reader, and communicative purpose. The profile is a
  formal professional text, so it needs a clear technical register without
  sounding like a translated list of technologies.
- [Instituto Cervantes: «Coherencia»](https://cvc.cervantes.es/ensenanza/biblioteca_ele/diccio_ele/diccionario/coherencia.htm)
  explains that coherence depends on the relationship between the text, its
  context, and its reader. The profile therefore presents one connected idea:
  the range of work first, followed by the technical interest and open-source
  practice.
- [Instituto Cervantes: «Expresión escrita»](https://cvc.cervantes.es/ensenanza/biblioteca_ele/diccio_ele/diccionario/expresionescrita.htm)
  recommends planning, organising, drafting, and revising written text. It
  also calls for conceptual and lexical precision, an appropriate register,
  and a clear distinction between main and supporting ideas. Its C2 reference
  describes clear, fluent, logically structured writing that helps the reader
  find the important ideas.
- [Instituto Cervantes: *La escritura académica: pautas*](https://cvc.cervantes.es/Ensenanza/biblioteca_ele/publicaciones_centros/PDF/napoles_2015/17_martin.pdf)
  identifies precision, clarity, and objectivity as useful properties of a
  specialised text and recommends precise terms to avoid ambiguity.
- [LinkedIn Help: Create a good LinkedIn profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a554351/how-do-i-create-a-good-linkedin-profile)
  says that the About section should express a person's mission, motivation,
  and skills. It recommends one or two paragraphs and keeping relevant work
  and skills aligned with the person's current goals.
- [LinkedIn Talent Solutions: 14 profile summaries and best practices](https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/product-tips/linkedin-profile-summaries-that-we-love-and-how-to-boost-your-own)
  recommends making the first sentence count, using relevant keywords,
  cutting jargon, writing in the first person, reading the text aloud, and
  keeping enough white space for scanning. These are guidance for a LinkedIn
  About section, so this CV uses the principles at a shorter scale.
- [Europass: Crear tu CV](https://europass.europa.eu/es/create-europass-cv)
  recommends adapting the CV to the target, focusing on relevant facts and
  main points, using clear and simple language, and choosing expressive verbs
  such as «gestionar» and «desarrollar». It also asks the writer to check
  grammar and spelling.
- [Universidad de La Laguna: Elaboración efectiva de tu currículum](https://www.ull.es/portal/agenda/evento/charla-de-orientacion-elaboracion-efectiva-de-tu-curriculum/)
  describes its university employment guidance as presenting the professional
  profile clearly and practically, and adapting the CV to the offer and the
  organisation. This is a direct local career-guidance reference for the
  audience and institution represented in the CV.
- [RAE: «software»](https://dle.rae.es/software)
  records *software* as a masculine Spanish noun in the computing sense. The
  word is therefore suitable for this technical CV.
- [FundéuRAE: «inteligencia artificial», con minúsculas](https://www.fundeu.es/recomendacion/inteligencia-artificial-ia-minusculas/)
  recommends lowercase for the common expression *inteligencia artificial*
  and uppercase for the abbreviation *IA*. The CV uses the English technical
  term *agentic AI*, as requested by its owner.
- [FundéuRAE: «agéntico / agentivo»](https://www.fundeu.es/consultas/palabra-clave/agentico/)
  records current uncertainty between *IA agentiva* and *IA agéntica*. The CV
  retains *agentic AI* rather than choosing a Spanish technical translation.

## Editorial decisions

The profile is a compact professional introduction, not a second project list.
It uses first-person present tense for current practice, two sentences, and
roughly 30–40 words. The first sentence establishes the engineering range;
the second states the area of interest and the open-source practice. The
experience bullets carry the project evidence.

The wording keeps technical terms in English: *software*, *stack*, *bare-metal*,
*frontend*, *agentic AI*, and *open source*. It avoids claims about users, revenue, performance, seniority,
or production adoption because the profile data does not establish them.

The selected wording describes the technical range with «desde bare-metal
hasta el frontend». Spanish carries the verbs and connections; English carries
the technical terms. The owner's terminology preference takes precedence over
Spanish translations in the language references. This is a choice for this CV,
rather than a rule prescribed by Cervantes or LinkedIn.

## Selected paragraph

> Desarrollo software en todo el stack, desde bare-metal hasta el frontend. Me interesa especialmente agentic AI y publico la mayor parte de mi trabajo como open source.

The first sentence gives the reader a clear technical range. The second
connects the current interest with the open-source practice. It uses direct
first-person verbs and avoids repeating the technology and project lists.
The English role names remain outside the translation dictionary.

## Factual boundary

The paragraph above uses only facts already present in the shared profile:
work across bare-metal systems and interfaces, a particular interest in
agentic AI, and mostly open-source work. The project-specific evidence remains
in the Spanish experience bullets and the linked projects.
